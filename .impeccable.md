
## Design Context

### Users
Primarily serious athletes, coaches, and gym-goers who take training seriously. Audience skews toward people who understand programming (percentages, training maxes, cycles) but may not be tech-savvy — many are used to writing sets and reps on paper. The app needs to feel immediately intuitive: no learning curve, no friction mid-workout when hands are chalked and rest time is ticking. Also used by coaches reviewing athlete data, so it needs to work at a desk, not just in a gym.

### Brand Personality
**Focused, exact, disciplined.**
The physical analog is a quality training journal — thick paper, precise ruled columns, nothing decorative that doesn't serve the record. Like Linear, it is a serious tool that treats the user as capable. Like Stripe, it earns trust through clear hierarchy and confident spacing. It does not try to motivate you. It just works.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark. Used in gyms, under harsh fluorescent lighting, with a phone in one hand and chalk on the other. Dark reduces glare and keeps focus on the data.
- **Color**: Deep near-black backgrounds with a tinted neutral palette. Single accent color — precise and rare, not sprinkled everywhere. Not electric-blue-on-dark (current palette is too generic "developer tool"). Consider a more restrained cool steel or a warm iron-oxide direction instead.
- **Typography**: Heading font with quiet authority and strong weight contrast — not sporty, not editorial fashion, not "fitness app." Body font optimized for legibility at small sizes (set numbers, weights, percentages). The type hierarchy should do the visual heavy lifting.
- **Density**: Information-dense without feeling cluttered. A paper log fits a lot on one page — the digital version should too. Avoid over-carding everything.
- **References**: Linear (precision, dark, fast, tool-first), Stripe (typographic confidence, information hierarchy, trust).
- **Anti-references**: MyFitnessPal (consumer-y, noisy), Strong app (functional but rough), Apple Fitness (lifestyle marketing). No motivational language, no emoji, no achievement badges.

### Design Principles
1. **Friction is the enemy.** Every tap should be the obvious next move. Mid-workout interactions must be one gesture.
2. **The data is the design.** Numbers — weights, percentages, sets — are the product. Typography and layout serve them; decoration doesn't compete.
3. **Analog clarity.** It should feel as easy to read as a well-organized paper notebook. Clear columns, strong contrast, consistent rhythm.
4. **Earn trust quietly.** No empty states with cute illustrations. No loading skeletons that flicker. Fast, confident, direct.
5. **Nothing without purpose.** Every visual element must earn its place. If removing it doesn't change the experience, it shouldn't be there.

### Typography Direction
- **Headings / labels / stats**: Barlow Semi Condensed — strong presence, slightly compressed geometry, works well at heavy weights without feeling sporty or gimmicky. Echoes the precision of calibration markings.
- **Body / inputs / data**: Hanken Grotesk — clean, precise, exceptional legibility at small sizes, not overused. Good at the 13–15px range where set data lives.

### Color Direction
Move away from pure hex values toward OKLCH. Tint all neutrals toward the brand hue. The accent should feel like precision steel or a single lit indicator — not electric or glowing. Explore: deep navy-charcoal base with a cool gray-blue tint, single accent in the 200–240° hue range but muted (chroma ~0.12–0.15 vs. current 0.25+).
